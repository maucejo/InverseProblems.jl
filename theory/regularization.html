Regularization

Regularization restores the well-posedness of the inverse problem by adding prior knowledge on the sources. In SourceEstimation.jl, a regularization is made of a regularization term 𝑅(𝑢) — the prior — and a formulation combining it with the data fidelity.

Regularization terms

ℓ𝑞 norms

The basic term is the ℓ𝑞 (quasi-)norm of a linear transform of the unknown (|⋅| is the absolute value for real data, the modulus for complex data),

(1)

implemented by Lq(q; L = I). The shape parameter 𝑞 encodes the nature of the sources [1]:

  • 𝑞=2 (Tikhonov) promotes distributed fields, with energy spread over many points;
  • 𝑞=1 (LASSO) promotes sparse fields, i.e. localized sources;
  • 𝑞<1 promotes even sparser fields, at the cost of a non-convex problem.

The operator 𝐿 is the identity by default. Finite-difference operators (diff_operator) penalize the derivatives of the field, i.e. promote smooth distributions. For a matrix unknown, 𝐿 acts on the rows (space): 𝐿𝑈.

Mixed norms

Lpq(2, 0.5) describes broadband (𝑝=2) and localized (𝑞=0.5) sources, Lpq(1, 2) narrow-band distributed ones. The operator 𝐿𝑐 acts on the columns, e.g. along the frequencies to promote continuous spectra. On vec(𝑈), the global operator is a Kronecker product,

Parameter choice rules

All the rules work on the spectral description of the weighted quadratic problem min𝑢‖𝑦−𝐻𝑢‖2+𝜆‖𝑊1/2𝐿𝑢‖2 solved at each iteration: SVD of 𝐻𝑊−1/2 if 𝐿=𝐼, generalized SVD (GSVD) of the pair (𝐻,𝑊1/2𝐿) otherwise. Let 𝛾𝑖 denote the (generalized) singular values and 𝛽𝑖 the coefficients of the data in the corresponding basis. The criteria are minimized in 𝑡=logℓ, with 𝜆=ℓ2, by a grid search refined by Brent’s method.

Tip
The multiplicative formulations require no parameter choice rule, but they still benefit from a good initial solution. By default, the IR algorithm starts from a Tikhonov solution whose parameter is given by the init keyword of solve.
