Algorithms

All the formulations of SourceEstimation.jl lead to the same computational kernel: a sequence of weighted Tikhonov problems. The data may be real or complex: with the notations of the first page (𝐴𝐻 is the transpose for real data, the conjugate transpose for complex data), all the formulas below hold in both cases. This page describes the iteratively reweighted algorithm, the resolution strategies of space–frequency and space–time problems, and the linear solvers.

Iteratively reweighted algorithm

The IR algorithm (IR()) is derived from the direct application of the first-order optimality condition of the regularized problem [1], [2]. It is the algorithm of the additive and multiplicative formulations, and the 𝑢-step of the Bayesian MAP.

Fixed point

At iteration 𝑘+1, with 𝑉=𝐿𝑈(𝑘):

  1. compute the local weights 𝑊 and the penalties 𝑃𝑔=𝑅𝑔(𝑉𝑔) of the groups;
  2. update the regularization parameter(s) — fixed, chosen by a rule, adaptive (𝛼 or 𝛼𝑔 of the multiplicative formulations) or given by the hyperparameters of the Bayesian model

  3. solve the weighted Tikhonov problem

    (1)

The iterations stop when the relative variation ‖𝑈(𝑘+1)−𝑈(𝑘)‖1/‖𝑈(𝑘)‖1 falls below tol, or after maxiter iterations (100 by default). The initial solution is a Tikhonov solution whose parameter is given by init: Heuristic() by default for the multiplicative formulations, the parameter (or its choice rule) of the formulation for the additive one.

Resolution strategies

The rows of the unknown are the points and the columns the samples. The strategy is chosen by multiple dispatch on the operator and on the coupling induced by the regularization terms:

CaseStrategyResolution
space–frequency, Lq onlyper sampleone independent problem per frequency, in parallel (own 𝛼, 𝜆 and convergence)
space–frequency, Lpq without Lcjoint (blocks)common weights and parameter, but 𝐻𝐻𝐻+𝐷 block-diagonal: frequency-by-frequency solves in parallel
space–time (dense), without Lcjoint (dense)dense system of size 𝑛𝑟𝑛𝑡, operator 𝐼⊗𝐿
Lpq with Lcassembledautomatically vectorized problem (sparse block-diagonal 𝐻, operator 𝐿𝑐⊗𝐿), sparse Cholesky

Without Lc, the block-diagonal structure is always exploited and the frequencies are only coupled through the weights. With Lc, the term 𝐿𝐻𝐷𝐿 links neighbouring frequencies (block-tridiagonal system for a 3-point operator) and the problem is solved as a whole with a sparse factorization.

Spectral decompositions

The parameter choice rules require the SVD of 𝐻𝑊−1/2 (𝐿=𝐼) or the GSVD of (𝐻,𝑊1/2𝐿). The components of the null space of 𝐿 are not regularized; they are accounted for in the criteria. The decompositions are computed on the standard-form problem, so that the cost per evaluation of a criterion is negligible.

Memory and allocations

A linear system object is built once per resolution. It precomputes everything that does not depend on the weights (𝐻𝐻𝐻, transpose of the global smoothing operator, standard form 𝐻𝐿−1) and preallocates its buffers; the fixed-point loop then works in place. The solvers are compiled for the concrete types of the problem (regime, smoothing operator, real or complex field) thanks to function barriers.
