Linear inverse problems

This page introduces the class of problems addressed by SourceEstimation.jl and explains why a naive inversion fails. The next pages describe the regularization strategies, their Bayesian interpretation, the algorithms and the posterior uncertainty.

Real or complex data

Depending on the problem, the data and the unknowns are real (time domain, static or real modal problems) or complex (frequency domain). SourceEstimation.jl handles both with the same code: the field is deduced from the element types of H and y (Float64 or ComplexF64), and the formulas that depend on it — essentially the probability distributions of the Bayesian model — are selected by dispatch. A common layout

In both domains, the unknown is a matrix 𝑈∈𝕂𝑛𝑟×𝑛𝑠 whose rows are the reconstruction points (space) and whose columns are the samples (frequencies or time steps); a single-frequency problem has 𝑛𝑠=1. Global vectors are stacked sample by sample, 𝑢=vec(𝑈), i.e. 𝑢𝑖+(𝑗−1)𝑛𝑟=𝑈𝑖𝑗. This convention gives the same meaning to groups, smoothing operators and mixed norms in all domains.

Singular value decomposition

Source identification problems are discrete ill-posed problems [3]: the singular values decay gradually towards zero, so that the condition number 𝜎1/𝜎𝑟 is very large. The noise components 𝑣𝑖𝐻𝑛 do not decay, and the second sum of Equation 5 dominates the solution: the least-squares estimate is useless, even with a very low noise level.

The discrete Picard condition

A meaningful solution can only be recovered if the Fourier coefficients of the exact data |𝑣𝑖𝐻𝐻𝑢| decay faster than the singular values — the discrete Picard condition. Above the noise level, the coefficients |𝑣𝑖𝐻𝑦| level off and the corresponding components must be filtered out. This is the role of regularization: it replaces Equation 5 by a filtered expansion

Noise

The noise is assumed Gaussian and centered — real Gaussian for real data, circular complex Gaussian for complex data — with a known diagonal covariance 𝑅 (real, positive) (up to a scale factor, see the Bayesian formulation). The data fidelity is then the weighted least-squares term

(7)

It is handled once and for all by whitening: 𝐻←𝑅−1/2𝐻, 𝑦←𝑅−1/2𝑦, after which the noise covariance is the identity. In SourceEstimation.jl, 𝑅 is given by the keyword R of solve: σ² * I, the variances of all the data, or one variance per measurement point shared by all the samples.
